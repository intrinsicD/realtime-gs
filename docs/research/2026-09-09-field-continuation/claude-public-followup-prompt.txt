## Framing

Two claims need separating up front.

**[MATH]** *Site-exactness lemma.* If the teacher field $T$ and the photograph $I$ agree exactly at every site the loss reads — pixel centres, plus every pixel entering each SSIM window, after the identical clip/gamma/premultiply/mask pipeline — then for any frozen student state $\theta_0$, $L(\theta_0;T)=L(\theta_0;I)$ and $\nabla_\theta L$ is identical, element-wise. Nothing about optics is involved; it's substitution. The useful direction is the contrapositive: divergence *localises* a disagreement at specific sites, or a mismatch in the operator, and nowhere else.

**[MATH]** *The converse fails.* Small $\|T-I\|_\infty$ bounds neither $\|\theta^*_T-\theta^*_I\|$ nor novel-view error. The data→argmin map has local sensitivity $\sim\|H^{-1}\|$, and 3DGS's Hessian is near-singular along opacity→0, scale→0, and the opacity/scale/SH trade-off directions. A 1/255 RGB perturbation can move parameters arbitrarily far along a flat direction. So "the field is 45 dB PSNR" is not an argument, and neither is "the reconstruction got worse, therefore the field lost 3D information."

**[MATH]** Two mechanisms make the *predicted* boundary/opacity signature unsurprising without any information loss about geometry. (a) SSIM at pixel $p$ depends on local mean, variance and covariance over an 11×11 window; a field fit that is locally variance-deficient across an edge perturbs the contrast/structure sub-terms *even where mean RGB matches*, and 3DGS weights this at $\lambda=0.2$ **[LIT, verified]**. (b) 2D Gaussians have unbounded support, so colour leaks across the silhouette; composited against a fixed background, the leaked pixel is a mixture the student can only match by placing partially-opaque geometry — a halo of excess opacity is the *correct* solution to the stated objective. Add **[LIT]** renderer-side boundary error that afflicts the photograph arm too: per-splat single-depth sorting causes popping/blending errors (StopThePop), and 3DGS aliasing arises from the missing 3D frequency constraint plus the 2D dilation filter (Mip-Splatting). A field teacher is band-limited by *its* fit, not by the sensor or the renderer's filter.

## First-stage experiments (≤4, ranked by diagnostic value)

**E1 — Frozen-state residual and gradient audit. [PROPOSAL]** Yes, this is the right first test, and it is nearly free. At a common frozen $\theta_0$, recompute both residuals; report $\Delta L$ decomposed into the L1 term and the SSIM luminance/contrast/structure sub-terms, stratified into silhouette-distance bands; report $\|\nabla_\theta L\|$ per parameter group. Run alongside it a **pipeline-identity control**: push the *photograph* through the teacher's exact I/O path with the fit replaced by identity. If that control alone reproduces part of the gap, the finding is an operator bug (resampling, premultiplication, colour space, mask alignment), not a claim about fields.

**E2 — 2D-only teacher-error localisation. [PROPOSAL]** Teacher-minus-photo maps in the training colour space, stratified by silhouette distance and by frequency band, plus the 11×11 local-variance ratio. No 3D optimisation. This asks whether the boundary deficit already exists in 2D before any reconstruction.

**E3 — Fixed-geometry appearance-only refit. [PROPOSAL]** Freeze positions/scales/rotations from a converged photo run; optimise colour and opacity only, against each target. Excess opacity appearing *here* is target-driven, not densification-driven.

**E4 — Optimiser/topology variance band. [PROPOSAL]** Photo target, multiple seeds × densification on/off. Densification is threshold-triggered ($\tau_{pos}=0.0002$) with periodic opacity reset to near-zero every 3000 iterations **[LIT, verified]** — discontinuous in the data. Without this band, no field-vs-photo delta is interpretable.

**Decision tree.** E1 pipeline-identity control non-null → fix operator, restart. Else if E1 gradients are close *and* E2 shows a boundary-localised teacher deficit → teacher boundary/background error; the target, not the reconstruction, is the object of study. Else if E1 gradients are close and E2 is null but full runs diverge → trajectory/topology sensitivity; check against E4's band before calling it real. Else if E3 already shows excess opacity → appearance/compositing, and geometry ambiguity is not yet implicated. Only if E1–E4 all come back null-or-small is "geometry/visibility ambiguity" a live hypothesis, and it is the *last* explanation, not the first.

## Masks and clips (point 2)

Three distinct things. Scoring-only masks (never in the loss) are legitimate stratification and belong in E1–E4. **Source-mask supervision is an oracle**: it injects silhouette information the colour field does not carry, so it converts the task rather than diagnosing it. A hard 3D containment clip is worse — it can hide false opacity and delete genuine protruding geometry in the same operation, and it makes the photo baseline non-comparable. **[PROPOSAL]** Run both oracle arms, but last, labelled as upper bounds, with the paired non-oracle arm always reported beside them. Never as a headline number.

## Baseline hygiene (point 3)

Split cameras into *dev* and *confirm* before anything. All photo-baseline development on dev only; the confirm set is touched once per frozen protocol version and is then burned. Field targets are retested with the identical frozen recipe — no re-tuning. Report three regimes separately, because they answer different questions: fixed-geometry appearance-only (E3), fixed-topology full-parameter, adaptive topology. **[MATH/terminology]** Do not read 3DGS opacity as material density or as teacher alpha; it is a per-primitive blending weight under a sorted alpha compositor, gauge-equivalent under scale/SH trade-offs.

## Tomography control (point 4)

**[MATH]** Under orthographic projection the line integral of a 3D Gaussian is exactly a 2D Gaussian with $\Sigma_{2D}=P\Sigma P^\top$ and preserved mass — so a known 3D GMM has an analytic sinogram. **[PROPOSAL]** (a) additive forward model, dense angles → should recover; (b) limited angular range → **[LIT]** Frikel–Quinto invisible singularities and end-of-range streaks are the *expected* failure, and observing them validates the harness; (c) swap to emission–absorption/opaque compositing and measure the collapse. **[LIT]** R²-Gaussian reports a previously unknown integration bias in the standard 3DGS formulation for volume retrieval, traced to a covariance-related scaling factor in the 3D→2D projection — i.e. the same primitives need *different* normalisation under an additive operator, which is precisely the point of the control. **[LIT]** Under perspective the projection is not Gaussian; EWA's local affine (Jacobian) approximation is what makes it tractable, and its error grows off-axis and with kernel extent. **What success proves:** your parameterisation and optimiser can invert an additive line-projection operator when the operator is correct. **What it cannot prove:** anything about occlusion, view-dependent colour, or that photographs carry CT-like information. A same-renderer round trip is self-consistency only — score against the analytic GMM and an independent voxel forward model, or the result is vacuous.

## Field-only geometry (point 5)

**[LIT]** Finite Gaussian mixtures are identifiable as densities (Yakowitz–Spragins), but the local minimax rate for an over-specified mixture is $n^{-1/(4(m-m_0)+2)}$ (Heinrich–Kahn) — components are *practically* non-identifiable under over-parameterisation. Independent per-view fits therefore give components with no reliable cross-view meaning, which undercuts ray-space footprint lifting at its foundation.

**[PROPOSAL]** Smallest informative comparison: Arm A, decode patches and run standard calibrated multiview matching; Arm B, footprint lifting; Control C, conventional MVS on the real photographs. Prohibited in a field-only arm: original photographs, source masks, any depth or pose beyond the supplied calibration, and any model trained on this scene. Monocular depth priors are a *separate declared arm*, not field-only. **Abandonment rule:** if B fails to beat A on the confirm set after two protocol revisions, drop component-level lifting.

## Attacking E1

E1's lemma is decisive only under exact equality; in the near-equality case, matched gradients at one state say nothing about a trajectory dominated by discontinuous densification events. E1 can come back "nearly identical" and the runs still diverge — that is a real possible outcome, not a bug. Worse, the choice of $\theta_0$ biases it: at a photo-converged state the photo residual is ~zero by construction. Use a neutral third state and report both.

**Non-identifiable regardless:** whether excess opacity is false geometry or a gauge-equivalent re-encoding of the same radiance field (only novel-view renders are comparable, not opacity maps); whether boundary error is teacher-side or rasteriser-side, without swapping the rasteriser; and any general claim about "information in 2D Gaussian fields" — you can only ever measure a property of one fitting procedure.
