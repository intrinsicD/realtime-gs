\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{3D Gaussian splatting}
3DGS \citep{kerbl2023_3dgs} represents a scene as anisotropic 3D Gaussians rendered by
EWA-style splatting \citep{zwicker2001_ewa} with front-to-back alpha compositing and
optimizes them against source photographs with adaptive densification.  Follow-up work
improves the densification and optimization machinery itself, for example through
gradient-magnitude corrections~\citep{ye2024_absgs}, pixel-coverage-aware
growth~\citep{zhang2024_pixelgs}, throughput-oriented
re-engineering~\citep{mallick2024_taming} and a reformulation of densification as MCMC
sampling~\citep{kheradmand2024_mcmc}.  We change none of this machinery.  The manipulated
variables in our approach are the supervision representation and the initialization.

\subsection{2D Gaussian image representations}
GaussianImage \citep{zhang2024_gaussianimage} fits images with 2D Gaussians for fast
decoding and compression and shows that a few thousand anisotropic 2D Gaussians represent
a photograph at high fidelity.  In our approach the fitted field is not a compression
endpoint but the sole supervision signal for the 3D optimization, which requires exact,
self-describing evaluation semantics, see \cref{sec:fields}.  This is distinct from 2D
Gaussian surfel splatting \citep{huang2024_2dgs}, which changes the 3D scene primitive.
Our 2D Gaussians live in image space and describe input photographs.

\subsection{Compressed 3DGS}
A separate line of work compresses the output model through vector quantization and
pruning~\citep{lee2024_compact3dgs,fan2024_lightgaussian} or entropy-coded
representations~\citep{niedermayr2024_compressed}.  These methods are orthogonal to ours.
They compress the final model, while we compress the input side of the optimization,
namely the supervision working set held in GPU memory during training.  Both can be
combined.

\subsection{Initialization}
Standard 3DGS initializes from a sparse COLMAP
reconstruction~\citep{schoenberger2016_colmap}.  Random initialization degrades quality or
slows convergence, which RAIN-GS~\citep{jung2024_raings} mitigates by schedule design.
Dense learned priors like DUSt3R~\citep{wang2024_dust3r} and
MASt3R~\citep{leroy2024_mast3r} power InstantSplat-style
pipelines~\citep{fan2024_instantsplat} which initialize from predicted dense geometry.
Our tomographic initializer differs in its input.  It consumes no images and no learned
prior at reconstruction time, only the fitted 2D Gaussian fields and calibration, and it
produces contributor lineage as a by-product, see \cref{sec:tomography}.  Space
carving~\citep{kutulakos2000_carving} inspires the image-free placement control used in
our ablation study, and covariance intersection~\citep{julier1997_ci} supplies the fusion
rule together with its consistency guarantee under unknown correlation.
