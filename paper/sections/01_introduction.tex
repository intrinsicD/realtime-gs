\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

3D Gaussian Splatting \citep{kerbl2023_3dgs} optimizes an explicit set of anisotropic 3D
Gaussians against photographs by differentiable rasterization.  The optimization is
image-hungry by construction: every iteration samples one or more source views and compares a
full render against the stored photograph.  The consequence is easy to overlook on benchmark
scenes and dominant on real capture rigs: \emph{the source images themselves are the largest
resident data structure of the reconstruction}.  A camera dome producing tens to hundreds of
high-resolution views per frame quickly exceeds what a single GPU can hold, and streaming the
images from disk trades the memory bottleneck for a storage-bandwidth bottleneck.  The model
being optimized --- a few hundred thousand Gaussians --- is tiny by comparison.

This paper asks whether the photographs need to be there at all.  Our observation is that 3DGS
does not consume images; it consumes \emph{colors at projected sample locations}.  Any
representation that can answer point queries ``what color does view $v$ see at image location
$\bm{x}$?'' can supervise the optimization.  We therefore fit every input image once, offline,
with a compact mixture of anisotropic 2D Gaussians --- a \emph{compact capture} --- and discard
the image.  The capture is three orders of magnitude smaller than the raw RGB working set
(\cref{sec:vram-protocol}), analytic, differentiable, and can be evaluated at arbitrary
subpixel locations without decoding, resampling, or caching full frames.

The resulting pipeline has a hard, checkable data boundary: after the per-view fit, no stage of
reconstruction opens an image file, imports an image loader, or receives a dense RGB tensor.
Reconstruction consumes only calibrated cameras plus frozen 2D Gaussian fields.  We treat this
process separation as part of the claim rather than an implementation preference, and we
enforce it structurally (typed inputs, import denials, containment invariants;
\cref{sec:setting}).\evidence{ara/logic/claims.md C31}

\subsection{Two claims, firewalled}
\label{sec:two-claims}

The paper is organised as two deliberately separated parts, because it contains two claims of
different kinds and different risk profiles, and because we predeclare that the failure of the
second must not contaminate the first.

\begin{claimstmt}[Systems / VRAM claim --- Part I]
\label{claim:vram}
Compact 2D Gaussian captures contain sufficient information to supervise reconstruction and
refinement of a standard 3D Gaussian Splatting representation without returning to source RGB.
The practical benefit is reconstruction of datasets whose raw RGB working set does not fit in
GPU memory.  The claim is established only by (i) held-out quality parity with a matched
RGB-backed control and (ii) a controlled reduction in \emph{measured} peak VRAM; the structural
absence of RGB alone is insufficient.
\end{claimstmt}

\begin{claimstmt}[Beam Fusion hypothesis --- Part II]
\label{claim:beam}
Given the same compact captures, Beam Fusion --- a closed-form tomographic 3D initializer with
carrier refinement --- may provide a better 3D initialization than the no-Beam arm, improving
time-to-quality, final held-out quality, geometry, or required 3D capacity at matched compute.
\end{claimstmt}

Part~I is tested by a \emph{no-Beam} arm: a direct compact reconstruction whose initializer,
optimizer, topology budget, and stopping rule match the RGB-backed control exactly, so the only
manipulated variable is the supervision representation.  Part~II is exploratory; its decision
rule (\cref{sec:exp2-decision}) predeclares that a Beam tie or loss is reported as a bounded
negative result and is not folded into the systems claim.  This firewall is the load-bearing
design decision of the paper: a systems paper wearing a science costume --- or the reverse ---
is the classic shape of a rejection.

\subsection{Contributions}
\label{sec:contributions}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Compact capture representation and compact-field supervision}
        (\cref{sec:compact-capture,sec:compact-supervision}).  A self-describing frozen 2D
        Gaussian field format with exact evaluation semantics, plus an unbiased point-sampled
        training objective for 3DGS against such fields, including a sparse point rasterizer
        with verified parity against a dense reference renderer.
        \emph{Status: implemented; mechanism-level properties are test- and audit-backed.}
  \item \textbf{Measured memory/quality trade-off of direct compact reconstruction}
        (\cref{sec:vram-protocol,sec:exp1}).  A controlled dense-versus-compact resource
        protocol (NVML process peaks, fresh-process isolation, matched initialization, seeds,
        and stopping rules) and its results.
        \emph{Status: \toshow{protocol drafted; the experiment has not been run --- this is the
        main missing evidence of Part~I}.}
  \item \textbf{Beam Fusion} (\cref{sec:beam-fusion}).  A correspondence-free, closed-form,
        RGB-free 3D initializer: per-view 2D Gaussians are back-projected to elongated 3D
        beams, gated in closed form, fused by equal-weight covariance intersection, and reduced
        with lineage-preserving selection.  We give an observability result (two views leave
        one covariance coordinate unobservable) that fixes the minimum contributor count at
        three.  \emph{Status: implemented, deterministic, mechanism-tested.}
  \item \textbf{Compact-only carrier refinement} (\cref{sec:carrier}).  A renderer-aware
        symmetric covariance repair, a two-phase fixed-topology compact optimization schedule,
        and a strict projected-center containment invariant, selected by three preregistered
        paired-root ablations.  \emph{Status: single-scene development evidence;
        \toshow{held-out and fresh-scene replication pending}.}
\end{enumerate}

Throughout, statements carry their evidence maturity explicitly.  Black text is mechanism,
definition, or evidence-bound result (with the binding artifact referenced); red text is an
obligation --- something this draft asserts we must still show, measure, freeze, or verify.
\Cref{sec:obligations} collects all obligations in one tracking table.

\begin{ToShowBlock}[Headline numbers still missing]
The abstract-level numbers of Part~I --- the held-out parity gap to RGB-backed training, the
measured peak-VRAM reduction factor $k$, and the maximum simultaneously-held view count at
fixed VRAM --- do not exist yet.  They require the controlled experiment of
\cref{sec:vram-protocol} on at least one outcome-unseen calibrated scene plus standard
benchmarks.  Until then this draft argues the mechanism and the protocol, not the result.
\end{ToShowBlock}
