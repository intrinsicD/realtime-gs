\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Reconstructing 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) models from dense multi-camera captures is
limited by the source images.  The optimization keeps every photograph available and
renders against stored images in each iteration, so the raw RGB working set quickly exceeds
GPU memory while the model itself stays small.  We propose to fit every input image once,
offline, with a compact per-view 2D Gaussian mixture, discard the images, and run all of 3D
reconstruction against the frozen 2D Gaussian fields.  These compact captures are small,
analytic and differentiable and can be queried at arbitrary image points, yielding a
streaming supervision for the 3D optimization.  We first show that a standard 3DGS
optimizer, initialized from random points or from a sparse SfM point cloud, reconstructs a
3D Gaussian field from the 2D fields alone.  \todo{Show held-out quality parity with
RGB-backed training and measure the reduction in peak GPU memory under the controlled
protocol of \cref{sec:memory}.  These headline numbers do not exist yet.}
Since rendering projects a 3D Gaussian field to per-view 2D Gaussians, the captures are
projections of the unknown scene and a 3D initialization can be obtained by inverting the
projection.  We back-project each 2D Gaussian into an elongated 3D beam, intersect the
beams across views in closed form and fuse them by covariance intersection.  The naive
inversion fails in identifiable ways.  We analyze these failures and address them with a
renderer-aware covariance repair, a fixed-topology refinement schedule and a strict
silhouette containment rule.  \todo{Show that this tomographic initialization improves
over the random and SfM baselines under matched budgets, supported by the ablation study
of \cref{sec:ablations}.}
All components are implemented, deterministic on CPU and covered by an audited evidence
ledger.  Current quantitative support is single-scene development evidence and is labelled
as such throughout.
\end{abstract}
